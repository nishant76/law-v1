> **Winning Argument:** The respondent succeeded because the appellant failed to prove the suit property’s exact identity, dimensions, boundaries, and the respondent’s alleged illegal possession, with the appellant’s own GPA/lease documents contradicting the plaint and no demarcation report produced.

> **Key Takeaway:** A plaintiff seeking possession and injunction must prove his own title/entitlement and the precise identity of the disputed property by reliable evidence, and cannot succeed on alleged weaknesses in the defendant’s case.

# Executive Brief

## Result

- **Col.R.P.S. Brar** succeeded; the appeal filed by **Anubhav Walia** was **dismissed**.
- The trial court’s dismissal of the plaintiff’s suit was upheld.
- Costs were awarded against the appellant; counsel fee was assessed at **Rs.2,500/-**.
- No interest direction was passed.

## Core Issue

- Whether the appellant proved that he was legally entitled to possession of the disputed land and that **Col.R.P.S. Brar** was in illegal possession of the same identified property.

## Key Facts

- The appellant claimed leasehold rights over land allegedly originating from a **99-year lease** granted by **Dera Garib Dass Ji** through its Mohitmim to **Narata Ram**.
- The appellant’s case was that after Narata Ram’s death, his legal heirs executed powers of attorney enabling further lease transactions.
- The appellant relied on a lease/power of attorney chain involving **Gurcharan Singh** and claimed entitlement to recover possession of land shown in **green colour** in the site plan.
- The appellant alleged that **Col.R.P.S. Brar**, a retired Army officer, had wrongfully taken possession, raised construction, and let out portions to other defendants.
- The respondent disputed the appellant’s title and identification of the land, contending that the appellant’s documents related to **property No.8135/5 / khasra No.429/2**, while the respondent’s property fell in **khasra No.429/1**.
- The respondent claimed lawful possession under independent lease documents and revenue entries.
- The plaintiff examined himself and a draftsman; the respondent examined multiple witnesses and produced documentary evidence.
- The suit had earlier been dismissed by the Civil Judge, Junior Division, Patiala, leading to this first appeal.

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** The appellant failed to prove the exact identity, dimensions, and boundaries of the suit property.  
  **Evidence:** The court compared the plaint, **GPA Ex.P18**, **Mark-A**, and the site plan, finding that the dimensions and directions did not tally.  
  **Impact:** The appellant could not establish that the land claimed by him was the same land occupied by the respondent.

- **Finding:** The appellant’s foundational title/entitlement documents did not support his pleaded case.  
  **Evidence:** The court noted that **Mark-A** referred to a backside plot, while the appellant claimed a front portion abutting the main road; the documents also lacked khasra/khewat/khatauni particulars.  
  **Impact:** The chain of authority through **Gurcharan Singh** was held insufficient to support the claim for possession.

- **Finding:** Illegal possession by the respondent was not proved.  
  **Evidence:** No demarcation report was produced, and the appellant failed to establish the date, month, or year when possession was allegedly taken.  
  **Impact:** The claim for possession and injunction could not be granted.

- **Finding:** The appellant’s oral evidence was materially unreliable on property identification.  
  **Evidence:** In cross-examination, **Anubhav Walia** admitted that relevant property particulars were absent from the power of attorney and that corrections were not supported by separate documents.  
  **Impact:** The court treated the appellant’s testimony as insufficient to prove the pleaded property description.

- **Finding:** The draftsman’s site plan did not reliably establish the disputed land.  
  **Evidence:** **PW2 Indresh Khanna** admitted he had not entered/measured the green-coloured area and had not shown complete details such as the uncovered area.  
  **Impact:** The site plan could not prove either the property’s dimensions or the respondent’s alleged encroachment.

- **Finding:** The trial court had properly appreciated the evidence.  
  **Evidence:** The appellate court reviewed the oral and documentary record and found no illegality, ambiguity, or perversity in the trial court’s judgment.  
  **Impact:** No appellate interference was warranted.

## What This Case Means

- **For Property Litigants:** A possession suit will fail if the plaintiff cannot precisely identify the land through consistent documents, reliable boundaries, and, where necessary, demarcation evidence.
- **For Defendants in Land Disputes:** Demonstrating inconsistencies in the plaintiff’s title chain and property description can be decisive even without needing to disprove every pleaded allegation.
- **For Civil Litigators:** Powers of attorney, lease deeds, site plans, and plaint descriptions must align on khasra numbers, boundaries, and dimensions before seeking possession or injunction.
