> **Winning Argument:** The appellant failed to prove the suit property’s correct identification (dimensions/directions) and the respondent’s alleged illegal possession because the key GPA/lease documents were internally inconsistent and there was no demarcation evidence.

> **Key Takeaway:** In a possession suit based on lease/GPA rights, the plaintiff must precisely identify the property (consistent boundaries/dimensions and ideally demarcation); courts will not grant possession/injunctions where foundational title/authority documents and property description do not match.

# Executive Brief

## Result

- **Appeal dismissed**: **Anubhav Walia** (appellant/plaintiff) lost; the trial court’s **judgment and decree dated 04.03.2020** dismissing the suit were **upheld**.
- **Costs imposed** on appellant; **counsel’s fee assessed at ₹2,500**.

## Core Issue

- Whether the appellant proved entitlement to possession/mandatory and permanent injunctions by establishing (i) a valid lease/right through the claimed chain of authority and (ii) **clear, consistent identification of the suit property** allegedly under respondent’s illegal occupation.

## Key Facts

- The appellant sued claiming leasehold rights over land stated to be **11 biswa / 525 sq. yards** at **Stadium Road, Patiala**, shown **green** in the site plan, and sought:
  - possession,
  - removal of alleged unauthorized structures (mandatory injunction),
  - restraint on rent collection (permanent injunction),
  - and claimed use/occupation charges (amounts pleaded).
- Appellant’s case rested on the asserted chain: Dera land allegedly leased (99 years) to **Narata Ram** (registered lease deed referenced), then devolving to Narata Ram’s heirs; they allegedly authorized attorneys who then created further lease/GPA arrangements culminating in a **GPA/lease arrangement dated 31.08.2005** linked to appellant.
- Respondent No.1 (**Col. R.P.S. Brar**) contested maintainability and asserted possession through a different chain of lease deeds/mutations and claimed appellant could not link **property No. 8135/5 / khasra 429/2** to respondent’s property stated to fall in **khasra 429/1**.
- **Respondent No.2 proceeded ex parte** in appeal; the appeal was contested mainly by **respondent No.1**.
- Plaintiff evidence: **PW1 Anubhav Walia** and **PW2 Indresh Khanna (Draftsman)**; documents **Ex.P1 to Ex.P20**, plus **Mark-D, Mark-E**, and in rebuttal **Ex.P21, Ex.P22**.
- Defence evidence: **DW1 Harinderjit Singh Sodhi**, **DW2 Gurinderjit Singh Sodhi**, **DW3 Col. R.P.S. Brar**, **DW4 HC Jaspal Singh**; documents **Ex.D1 to Ex.D67** and **Ex.D68 to Ex.D76**.
- The trial court had dismissed the suit on **04.03.2020**; this appeal challenged that dismissal.

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** The appellant did not prove the suit property’s correct **dimensions/directions/description**, so the property claimed could not be reliably identified for a decree of possession/injunction.
  **Evidence:** The court compared the plaint description with **Ex.P18 (GPA dated 31.08.2005 by Gurcharan Singh in appellant’s favour)** and **Mark-A (power of attorney executed by LR(s) of Narata Ram in favour of Gurcharan Singh)** and found **mismatching dimensions/directions**, and absence of **khasra/khewat/khatauni** particulars in the foundational documents.
  **Impact:** Failure of identification was treated as fatal to the appellant’s entitlement to possession/mandatory/permanent injunctions.

- **Finding:** The appellant’s foundation documents did not support authority over the **front portion abutting the main road**.
  **Evidence:** The court noted **Mark-A** depicted the plot given to Gurcharan Singh as a **“backside”** plot, and illegal possession by respondent was not depicted in **Mark-A**.
  **Impact:** Undermined appellant’s claim that Gurcharan Singh could lawfully create rights over the specific disputed portion claimed in the plaint/site plan.

- **Finding:** Absence of **demarcation** meant illegal possession could not be established on record.
  **Evidence:** The court recorded that no demarcation was conducted before filing suit; an application for demarcation was dismissed and no appeal was filed, so the order became final.
  **Impact:** Without demarcation, the court would not accept that respondent No.1 encroached upon/occupied the exact land claimed.

- **Finding:** Appellant’s oral testimony had material contradictions and admissions undermining his case.
  **Evidence:** PW1 admitted (as recorded) that the relied-on power of attorney lacked khasra/khewat/khatauni details; corrections were claimed but no correction document was produced; and he could not give rational answers on boundaries/directions vis-à-vis the documents.
  **Impact:** Reduced evidentiary value of appellant’s assertions; appellant failed to discharge the burden as plaintiff.

- **Finding:** The site plan evidence did not reliably prove measurements/possession.
  **Evidence:** PW2 (draftsman) admitted he had not entered the suit property for measurement, had not shown/checked title deed **property No. 8135/5**, and omitted key calculation particulars; the court treated the site plan/report as incomplete.
  **Impact:** The site plan could not establish the exact area allegedly in illegal possession of respondent No.1.

- **Finding:** Non-examination of the “best” witnesses (LRs of Narata Ram) weakened appellant’s case.
  **Evidence:** The court stated the LRs of Narata Ram could have brought truth on record, but appellant did not examine them.
  **Impact:** Supported the conclusion that appellant failed to prove entitlement and alleged illegal possession.

## What This Case Means

- **For property litigators (possession/injunction suits):** If your client’s claim depends on lease/GPA documents, ensure the **property description is consistent across plaint and all foundational instruments** and obtain a **demarcation report** where identity/encroachment is disputed.
- **For lessors/lessees in boundary disputes:** Long possession claims may succeed against a claimant who cannot precisely identify the disputed parcel or link documents to the claimed site.
