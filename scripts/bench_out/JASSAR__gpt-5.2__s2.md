> **Winning Argument:** The appellant failed to prove the suit property’s correct identity/dimensions and the respondent’s alleged illegal possession because the key GPAs/lease documents (Mark-A, **Ex.P18**) were internally inconsistent and no demarcation was proved.

> **Key Takeaway:** In a possession/mandatory injunction suit based on lease/GPA rights, the plaintiff must prove precise identification of the disputed land (dimensions/directions/location) with reliable documents (and, where contested on the ground, demarcation); oral assertions and an unverified site plan will not suffice.

# Executive Brief

## Result

- **Respondent No.1 (Col. R.P.S. Brar)** succeeded; the **appeal was dismissed** and the trial court’s **judgment and decree dated 04.03.2020** dismissing the suit were **upheld**.
- **Costs:** Appeal dismissed **with costs**; **counsel’s fee assessed at Rs.2,500**.

## Core Issue

- Whether the appellant proved a legally enforceable right to recover possession (and obtain mandatory/permanent injunctions and occupation charges) by correctly identifying the suit property and establishing the respondent’s illegal possession based on the appellant’s lease/GPA chain.

## Key Facts

- The appellant **Anubhav Walia** filed a civil suit claiming he was **lessee** of land (stated as **11 biswa / 525 sq. yards**, shown **green** in the site plan) at **Stadium Road, Patiala** and sought possession plus injunctions and use/occupation charges.
- Appellant’s pleaded chain: Dera Garib Dass Ji’s alleged lease to **Narata Ram** (99 years) → mutations/jamabandis → alleged legal heirs → **GPA to Gurcharan Singh** → **GPA/lease in favour of appellant (stated as 31.08.2005)**.
- Respondent No.1 disputed the appellant’s linkage, asserting the respondent’s property fell in a different khasra part and that the appellant’s documents pertained to **property No. 8135/5 / khasra 429/2**, not the respondent’s land.
- The trial court dismissed the suit by **judgment and decree dated 04.03.2020**.
- The appellant filed **CA CIS No.216 of 2020** (instituted **08.07.2020**), seeking reversal and decree of the suit.
- **Respondent No.2** was **ex-parte** in appeal.
- Evidence at trial (as recorded in appeal judgment): appellant examined **PW1 (self)** and **PW2 (draftsman)**; respondents examined **DW1, DW2, DW3 (Col. Brar), DW4** and produced documents **Ex.D1 to Ex.D76**.
- During the original suit, the appellant had initially sued multiple defendants; **defendants 2 to 4 were deleted** by order dated **25.03.2014** (as recorded in the judgment).

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** The appellant bore the burden to prove legal entitlement and illegal possession, and could not succeed on weaknesses in the defence.
  **Evidence:** The court applied the principle that “plaintiff is master of his own case” and must prove it with sufficient evidence.
  **Impact:** Set the lens for dismissal: failure of proof by appellant was decisive regardless of respondent’s case.

- **Finding:** The appellant failed to prove correct identification (dimensions/directions) of the suit property; the foundational documents were inconsistent.
  **Evidence:** The court compared **Ex.P18** with **Mark-A** and the plaint, noting mismatched dimensions/directions; **khasra/khewat/khatauni numbers were not mentioned** in key documents though pleaded in the plaint; **Mark-A** indicated a “backside plot” to Gurcharan Singh, not the front/main-road portion.
  **Impact:** The court held the appellant did not prove the suit property described in the plaint was the same property conveyed through his alleged chain, undermining the entire claim.

- **Finding:** Without a proved demarcation, illegal possession could not be accepted on the appellant’s oral assertions.
  **Evidence:** The judgment records there was **no demarcation report on file**; an application for demarcation was dismissed and **no appeal** was filed (order became final).
  **Impact:** The appellant could not reliably establish which exact land area was allegedly encroached/occupied by respondent No.1.

- **Finding:** Documentary evidence like jamabandis and mutations did not establish the suit property’s on-ground location/directions/dimensions.
  **Evidence:** The court held **Ex.P1**, **Ex.P3 to Ex.P8**, **Ex.P9 to Ex.P12**, **Ex.P13 to Ex.P15** only showed entries/inheritance but **not directions/dimensions**.
  **Impact:** These documents could not cure the identification defect or prove the respondent’s alleged illegal occupation of the specific plotted area.

- **Finding:** The appellant’s oral evidence was materially unreliable on critical particulars.
  **Evidence:** PW1 admitted: power of attorney relied upon had **no khasra/khewat/khatauni**, corrections were claimed but **no separate correction document** was produced; and he could not confirm whether khasra details were written in **Ex.P18**.
  **Impact:** The court treated these contradictions as fatal to proof of title/entitlement and property identity.

- **Finding:** The site plan evidence did not prove correct dimensions/illegal possession because it was inadequately prepared and unsupported.
  **Evidence:** PW2 (draftsman) admitted he had **no diploma**, **did not enter the suit property** to measure it, and omitted key calculations/annotations.
  **Impact:** The site plan **Ex.P16** was not accepted as reliable identification evidence.

## What This Case Means

- **For Property/Land Possession Plaintiffs:** If the defendant disputes identity/location, you must produce consistent title/lease/GPA documents that match the plaint description and support them with reliable identification evidence (including demarcation where necessary), otherwise the suit is likely to fail.
- **For Civil Trial/Appellate Practitioners:** Appeals attacking “perversity” will not succeed where the trial court’s dismissal is anchored in the plaintiff’s failure to prove foundational facts (property identification, competence in chain documents, and illegal possession).
