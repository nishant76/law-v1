> **Winning Argument:** UPSC’s advertisement expressly permitted shortlisting by higher relevant experience where applications were large, and the Court found that using enhanced experience to reduce the interview pool was neither arbitrary nor illegal.

> **Key Takeaway:** A selecting agency may apply a higher experience benchmark for shortlisting candidates, without changing eligibility criteria, if the recruitment rules or advertisement permit it and the benchmark is rational.

# Executive Brief

## Result

- **UPSC/respondents won**; the writ petitions were dismissed.
- The Court refused to interfere with the Central Administrative Tribunal’s rejection of interim relief against the interview process.
- The petitioner’s prayers to stay the interview schedule, keep one post vacant, or permit provisional participation were denied.
- No costs or interest directions were specified in the judgment.

## Core Issue

- Whether UPSC could validly shortlist candidates for interview by enhancing the required practice experience beyond the minimum eligibility stated in the recruitment advertisement.

## Key Facts

- **Manik Khurana**, a practising advocate, applied for the post of **Assistant District Attorney** in the Law and Prosecution Department, Chandigarh Administration.
- UPSC issued **Advertisement No. 08 of 2025 dated 28.06.2025** for **09 ADA vacancies**.
- The advertised minimum experience requirement was **two years’ practice** as an advocate.
- The advertisement included a clause stating that mere possession of minimum qualifications did not entitle candidates to interview.
- The advertisement also allowed UPSC, where applications were large, to adopt shortlisting criteria including **higher experience in the relevant field**.
- The petitioner submitted an online recruitment application on **08.07.2025**.
- UPSC received **1699 applications** for the advertised posts and shortlisted **138 applications** for the consideration zone.
- The petitioner approached the Central Administrative Tribunal after being excluded from interview consideration and then challenged the Tribunal’s order before the High Court.

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** UPSC’s shortlisting method was within the advertisement’s express terms.  
  **Evidence:** The advertisement permitted shortlisting by higher experience where the number of applications was large.  
  **Impact:** The Court held the petitioner could not claim an automatic right to be called for interview merely because he met minimum eligibility.

- **Finding:** Raising the experience benchmark did not alter the eligibility criteria.  
  **Evidence:** The Court relied on Supreme Court authority distinguishing minimum eligibility from rational shortlisting standards.  
  **Impact:** The challenge to the enhanced experience requirement failed.

- **Finding:** The benchmark adopted was not shown to be arbitrary or illegal.  
  **Evidence:** The shortlisting applied category-wise enhanced experience thresholds: **06 years** for unreserved/OBC candidates, **05 years** for EWS candidates, and reduced experience for PwBD candidates.  
  **Impact:** The Court found no infirmity in the selection agency’s administrative decision.

- **Finding:** The petitioner failed to satisfy interim relief requirements.  
  **Evidence:** The Court held that no prima facie case was made out and the balance of convenience was not in the petitioner’s favour.  
  **Impact:** The Tribunal’s refusal to stay the interview process was upheld.

- **Finding:** The connected petitions involved the same recruitment process and similar questions.  
  **Evidence:** The Court recorded that all three writ petitions arose from the same selection process.  
  **Impact:** The dismissal applied to the connected writ petitions as well.

## Authorities Relied Upon

- **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar, 1995 (1) SCT 50**  
  Principle: Shortlisting by higher experience is permissible where applications are numerous and selection is by interview, provided it is rational and does not change statutory eligibility.  
  Application: Supported UPSC’s decision to call only candidates with longer practice experience for interview.

- **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman, 1997 (4) SCC 664**  
  Principle: Benchmarks may be fixed at different recruitment stages if authorised by the rules or advertisement and not arbitrary.  
  Application: Reinforced the validity of applying a higher shortlisting threshold after receipt of applications.

- **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General, Supreme Court of India, 2025 NCDHC 7483**  
  Principle: Recruitment authorities may apply stage-wise benchmarks where permitted and legally non-arbitrary.  
  Application: Used to confirm that the UPSC benchmark was permissible under the advertisement.

## What This Case Means

- **For Recruiting Authorities:** A large applicant pool can justify rational shortlisting by enhanced experience where the advertisement reserves that power.
- **For Candidates:** Meeting minimum eligibility does not guarantee an interview if the advertisement permits further shortlisting.
- **For Service-Law Practitioners:** Challenges to recruitment shortlisting must show arbitrariness, illegality, or breach of the governing advertisement, not merely exclusion from consideration.
