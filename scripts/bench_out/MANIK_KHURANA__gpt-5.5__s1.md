## Core Issue

- Whether the UPSC could lawfully shortlist candidates for Assistant District Attorney interviews by raising the experience benchmark above the minimum eligibility requirement stated in the advertisement.

## Key Facts

- The petitioner, **Manik Khurana**, is a practising advocate who applied for the post of **Assistant District Attorney** in the Law and Prosecution Department, Chandigarh Administration.
- The UPSC issued **Advertisement No. 08 of 2025** for **9 ADA vacancies**.
- The advertised essential qualification required candidates to be qualified legal practitioners with **2 years’ practice**.
- The advertisement contained a shortlisting clause stating that mere possession of minimum qualifications would not entitle candidates to interview.
- The clause also allowed the Commission, where applications were large, to restrict interviews by methods including **higher experience in the relevant field**.
- The petitioner applied through an Online Recruitment Application and appeared in the list of applicants.
- The UPSC received **1699 applications** for the advertised posts and shortlisted **138 applications** for consideration.
- The petitioner approached the Tribunal seeking interim protection against exclusion from the interview process, but the Tribunal rejected the interim relief application.

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** Shortlisting by enhanced experience did not amount to changing the eligibility criteria.
  **Evidence:** The advertisement itself stated that minimum qualifications did not guarantee an interview and permitted shortlisting based on higher relevant experience.
  **Impact:** The petitioner’s core challenge to the revised interview benchmark failed.

- **Finding:** The UPSC had a rational basis to restrict the number of candidates called for interview.
  **Evidence:** The court relied on the large applicant pool compared with the limited number of advertised posts.
  **Impact:** The shortlisting exercise was treated as part of the selection process, not an arbitrary exclusion.

- **Finding:** Longer professional experience could legitimately be used as a selection-stage benchmark.
  **Evidence:** The court relied on Supreme Court authority recognising that candidates with longer practice may rationally be presumed to have greater maturity and experience.
  **Impact:** The enhanced experience requirement was upheld as legally permissible.

- **Finding:** The petitioner failed to establish a prima facie case.
  **Evidence:** The court found no infirmity in the respondents’ adoption of the shortlisting criteria under the advertisement.
  **Impact:** Interim or protective relief was refused.

- **Finding:** The balance of convenience did not favour intervention.
  **Evidence:** The recruitment process was already at the interview stage, and the Tribunal’s refusal of interim relief was found sustainable.
  **Impact:** The writ court declined to interfere with the Tribunal’s order.

## Authorities Relied Upon

- **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar, 1995 (1) SCT 50**
  - Principle: Shortlisting candidates by applying a higher experience threshold is permissible where large numbers apply and selection is by interview.
  - Application: Supported the UPSC’s power to call only more experienced candidates for interview without altering minimum eligibility.

- **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman, 1997 (4) SCC 664**
  - Principle: Recruitment authorities may set benchmarks at different stages if authorised and non-arbitrary.
  - Application: Reinforced the legality of stage-wise shortlisting through enhanced experience.

- **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General, Supreme Court of India, 2025 NCDHC 7483**
  - Principle: Employers can impose recruitment-stage benchmarks where rules or advertisement permit and the benchmark is not arbitrary or illegal.
  - Application: Applied to uphold the impugned shortlisting criteria used by the UPSC.

## What This Case Means

- **For Recruitment Authorities:** Express shortlisting clauses in advertisements can be relied on to manage large applicant pools by applying higher experience thresholds.

- **For Candidates:** Meeting minimum eligibility does not create an enforceable right to be called for interview where the advertisement permits further shortlisting.

- **For Service-Law Practitioners:** Challenges to shortlisting are unlikely to succeed unless the benchmark is unauthorised, irrational, arbitrary, or contrary to the governing rules.
