> **Winning Argument:** UPSC’s enhancement of the experience threshold for interview shortlisting was expressly permitted by the advertisement’s Note II (higher experience shortlisting) and was not shown to be arbitrary or illegal.
> **Key Takeaway:** A recruiting authority may set higher benchmarks at the shortlisting stage (including enhanced experience) where the rules/advertisement allow it and the benchmark is rational and non-arbitrary, without “changing” the minimum eligibility.

# Executive Brief

## Result

- **UPSC/Respondents won; petitioner lost.**
- **CWP No. 39633 of 2025** dismissed; consequently **CWP-39669-2025** and **CWP-39672-2025** also dismissed.
- No costs or interest directions are specified in the judgment.

## Core Issue

- Whether UPSC could lawfully **enhance the experience requirement for interview shortlisting** (from **2 years** to **6 years** for **UR/OBC** and **5 years** for **EWS**) under the recruitment advertisement.

## Key Facts

- UPSC issued **Advertisement No. 08 of 2025 dated 28.06.2025** for various posts including **9 posts of Assistant District Attorney (ADA)** for Chandigarh Administration.
- Essential experience in the advertisement: **Advocate with 2 years’ practice**.
- The advertisement contained Note provisions that mere possession of minimum qualifications does not entitle a candidate to interview, and that **shortlisting** may be adopted if applications are large, including **“higher experience”** as a basis.
- Petitioner **Manik Khurana** applied via **ORA dated 08.07.2025**.
- By **public notice dated 17.10.2025**, UPSC adopted an enhanced experience-based shortlisting criterion; candidates were asked to submit documents from **27.10.2025**.
- Petitioner made a **representation dated 22.10.2025** challenging the enhanced experience criterion; no action followed (as per pleadings).
- Petitioner filed **OA No. 1157 of 2025** before CAT, seeking stay of the interview process / permission to participate.
- CAT dismissed interim stay by **order dated 23.12.2025**; interviews were scheduled **29.12.2025 to 31.12.2025**.

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** Shortlisting by enhanced experience was contemplated by the recruitment advertisement and is legally permissible if non-arbitrary.
  **Evidence:** Advertisement Note II expressly allowed shortlisting “**on the basis of higher experience in the relevant field than the minimum prescribed**”.
  **Impact:** Undercut the petitioner’s core challenge that the experience increase was impermissible because it was not part of the original “essential qualification”.

- **Finding:** The shortlisting did not amount to altering the minimum eligibility; it was part of the selection process to manage large applications.
  **Evidence:** Supreme Court reasoning in **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar, 1995(1) SCT 50** (shortlisting by higher experience permissible to conduct meaningful interviews when applicant pool is large).
  **Impact:** Supported legality of calling only higher-experience candidates for interview despite lower statutory/advertised minimum.

- **Finding:** UPSC had a rational basis to invoke shortlisting given the applicant volume.
  **Evidence:** UPSC’s stand recorded in the judgment: **1699 applications** for **9 vacancies**; after applying the shortlisting criteria, **138 applications** were within the consideration zone.
  **Impact:** Helped establish non-arbitrariness and administrative necessity.

- **Finding:** The specific thresholds adopted (UR/OBC: **6 years**; EWS: **5 years**; PwBD: **1 year**) were not shown to be illegal or infirm.
  **Evidence:** UPSC’s written statement extract reproduced in the judgment setting out category-wise shortlisting benchmarks.
  **Impact:** Led directly to affirming CAT’s order and dismissal of the writ petitions.

- **Finding:** Petitioner failed on interim standards (prima facie case and balance of convenience).
  **Evidence:** Court’s express conclusion that neither a prima facie case nor balance of convenience was shown.
  **Impact:** No interference with CAT’s refusal of interim relief; writ dismissed.

- **Finding:** Employer may set benchmarks at different recruitment stages if rules/advertisement permit and it is not arbitrary/illegal.
  **Evidence:** **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman, (1997) 4 SCC 664**, and Delhi High Court decision following it: **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General Supreme Court of India, 2025 NCDHC 7483**.
  **Impact:** Reinforced the governing legal principle applied to uphold UPSC’s approach.

## Authorities Relied Upon

- **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar, 1995(1) SCT 50**
  - Principle: Shortlisting (including by higher experience) to limit interviewees is part of the selection process and does not change minimum eligibility, provided it is rational and non-arbitrary.
  - Application: Used to validate calling only higher-experience candidates for interview when applications far exceed vacancies.

- **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman, (1997) 4 SCC 664**
  - Principle: Employer can set benchmarks at different stages of recruitment if allowed by rules/advertisement and not arbitrary/illegal.
  - Application: Supported UPSC’s stage-wise benchmarking through shortlisting.

- **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General Supreme Court of India, 2025 NCDHC 7483**
  - Principle: Reiterates permissibility of stage-wise benchmarks where authorised and non-arbitrary.
  - Application: Cited as following Sundararaman; supports the same conclusion in this recruitment context.

## What This Case Means

- **For recruitment/selection challenges:** Courts will generally uphold experience-based shortlisting above the minimum eligibility where the advertisement expressly permits shortlisting by higher experience and the criterion is applied to manage large applicant pools.
- **For candidates excluded at shortlisting:** Attacks framed as “changing eligibility” are unlikely to succeed if the advertisement contains a shortlisting clause like Note II and the criterion is not shown to be arbitrary/illegal.
