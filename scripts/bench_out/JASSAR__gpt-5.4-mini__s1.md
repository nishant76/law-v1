# Executive Brief

## Result

- The **appellant, Anubhav Walia**, lost; the appeal was **dismissed with costs**.
- The trial court’s judgment and decree dated **04.03.2020** dismissing the suit were **upheld**.
- Costs were quantified as counsel’s fee of **Rs. 2,500/-**.
- No interest was ordered.

## Core Issue

- Whether the appellant had proved, by reliable documentary and oral evidence, that the **green-shaded suit property** was his leased property and that **Col. R.P.S. Brar** was in illegal possession.

## Key Facts

- The suit concerned land/property at **Stadium Road, Patiala**, which the appellant said formed part of a lease traceable to **Dera Garib Dass** through a **1962** lease in favour of **Narata Ram**.
- The appellant claimed that Narata Ram’s legal heirs later authorized transfers/leases through powers of attorney, culminating in a lease in favour of the appellant.
- The appellant alleged that **Col. R.P.S. Brar** had forcibly taken possession, constructed the building, and let portions to other occupants.
- The respondents denied the appellant’s title and asserted that the appellant was mixing up two different parcels of land.
- The respondents’ case was that the disputed property fell in a different khasra portion and that the appellant’s documents did not match the land in their possession.
- The trial court dismissed the suit on **04.03.2020**.
- On appeal, respondent No.2 remained **ex parte**.

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** The appellant failed to prove the suit property with certainty.
  **Evidence:** The court relied on the mismatch between the dimensions/directions in **Mark-A**, **Ex.P18**, and the plaint, and the absence of a reliable demarcation report.
  **Impact:** Without precise identification of the land, the claim for possession could not succeed.

- **Finding:** The appellant’s own title chain was internally inconsistent.
  **Evidence:** The court noted that the power of attorney and lease documents relied upon did not consistently mention the same property description, khasra details, or boundaries.
  **Impact:** The foundational documents did not establish a clear right in the appellant’s favour.

- **Finding:** Oral evidence did not cure the documentary defects.
  **Evidence:** The court found material discrepancies in the appellant’s cross-examination and treated the draftsman’s site plan evidence as incomplete and unreliable.
  **Impact:** The court held that oral assertions could not prove illegal possession when the property itself was not clearly identified.

- **Finding:** The appellant failed to prove illegal possession by the respondent.
  **Evidence:** The court emphasized that no exact date, month, or year of alleged illegal possession was proved, and no revenue demarcation was placed on record.
  **Impact:** The possession claim was treated as unsubstantiated.

- **Finding:** The trial court had already properly appreciated the evidence.
  **Evidence:** The appellate court reviewed the record and agreed with the trial court’s assessment of the oral and documentary evidence.
  **Impact:** There was no basis to interfere with the decree dismissing the suit.

## What This Case Means

- **For Plaintiffs in property-possession suits:** You must prove the exact land identity, boundaries, and location before asking for possession or injunction.
- **For Defendants in boundary/title disputes:** A documented inconsistency in the plaintiff’s land description can be decisive.
- **For Service-Law Practitioners:** Not applicable.
- **For Civil litigators:** A demarcation report can be outcome-determinative where the dispute turns on whether two parcels are being confused.
- **For Real estate litigators:** A chain of lease documents is only useful if each link consistently describes the same property.
