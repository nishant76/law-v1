# Executive Brief

## Result

- The **respondents** succeeded; **CWP No. 39633 of 2025** was **dismissed**.
- The Court upheld the **Central Administrative Tribunal’s order dated 23.12.2025** refusing interim stay of the interview process.
- No costs or interest direction is specified in the judgment.
- By paragraph 13, the connected writ petitions **CWP No. 39669 of 2025** and **CWP No. 39672 of 2025** were also dismissed.

## Core Issue

- Whether the **UPSC** could validly **shortlist candidates for interview by enhancing the experience requirement** from **2 years** in the advertisement to **6 years** for **UR/OBC** and **5 years** for **EWS** candidates.

## Key Facts

- The petitioner, **Manik Khurana**, and others applied for the post of **Assistant District Attorney** in the **Law and Prosecution Department, Chandigarh Administration**.
- The recruitment advertisement was **Advertisement No. 08 of 2025 dated 28.06.2025**, which prescribed **2 years’ practice** as an advocate as the experience requirement.
- The advertisement also stated that if applications were large in number, the Commission could adopt shortlisting criteria, including higher experience.
- The petitioner submitted an online application on **08.07.2025** and his name appeared in the list of applicants.
- The UPSC received **1,699 applications** against **9 vacancies**.
- By public notice dated **17.10.2025**, the Commission adopted enhanced shortlisting criteria and limited the interview zone to **138 applications**.
- The petitioner challenged the enhancement as arbitrary and sought interim protection before the **Central Administrative Tribunal** in **O.A. No. 1157 of 2025**.
- The Tribunal rejected the interim stay application by order dated **23.12.2025**, leading to this writ petition.

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** The shortlisting benchmark did not unlawfully alter the advertised eligibility criteria.
  **Evidence:** The advertisement’s Note II expressly allowed shortlisting by higher experience when applications were large.
  **Impact:** The petitioner’s challenge failed at the threshold because the impugned action was within the advertisement framework.

- **Finding:** The Commission’s decision was rational because the number of applicants was exceptionally high.
  **Evidence:** The Court relied on the fact that **1,699 applications** were received for only **9 posts**.
  **Impact:** The Court treated the enhanced experience filter as a legitimate selection-management measure.

- **Finding:** The action was consistent with binding precedent permitting benchmark-setting at different recruitment stages.
  **Evidence:** The Court relied on **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar**, **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman**, and **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General, Supreme Court of India**.
  **Impact:** These authorities supported the proposition that rational shortlisting is part of selection, not a change in eligibility.

- **Finding:** The petitioner failed to show a prima facie case for interim relief.
  **Evidence:** The Court expressly recorded that no prima facie case or balance of convenience favoured the petitioner.
  **Impact:** The Tribunal’s refusal to stay the interview process was upheld.

## Authorities Relied Upon

- **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar, 1995 (1) SCT 50**
  - Principle: Shortlisting candidates by higher experience does not amount to changing the prescribed eligibility if the benchmark is rational and selection-related.
  - Application: Used to justify the UPSC’s decision to call only candidates with higher experience.

- **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman, (1997) 4 SCC 664**
  - Principle: An employer may set benchmarks at different recruitment stages if permitted by the rules/advertisement and not arbitrary.
  - Application: Direct authority for the validity of shortlisting through enhanced experience criteria.

- **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General, Supreme Court of India, 2025 NCDHC 7483**
  - Principle: Recruitment benchmarks may be raised at a selection stage if authorised and non-arbitrary.
  - Application: Followed to reinforce the legality of the Commission’s shortlisting method.

## What This Case Means

- **For Recruitment Authorities:** If the advertisement expressly permits shortlisting, a higher interview-stage benchmark can be defended where the applicant pool is unusually large.
- **For Candidates:** Meeting the advertised minimum qualification does not guarantee an interview if a lawful shortlisting clause is invoked.
- **For Service-Law Practitioners:** Challenges to shortlisting must show that the benchmark was outside the advertisement or arbitrary; mere exclusion is not enough.

## Operative Directions

- The **petition stands dismissed accordingly**.
- The connected matters, **CWP No. 39669 of 2025** and **CWP No. 39672 of 2025**, **also stand dismissed**.

## Immediate Next Steps

- Check whether the **advertisement text** expressly authorises shortlisting by higher experience in similar recruitments.
- In any challenge to shortlist criteria, focus on whether the benchmark **departs from the advertisement or rules**.
- For ongoing recruitments, identify whether the applicant pool size can justify a rational shortlisting method under the notice.
- If advising candidates, distinguish between **minimum eligibility** and **selection-stage shortlisting**.
- Verify whether any separate challenge remains pending in the underlying **O.A. No. 1157 of 2025** after the interim issue was rejected.
