# Executive Brief

## Result

- The appeal by **Anubhav Walia** was **dismissed with costs** by the Additional District Judge, Patiala.
- The judgment and decree dated **04.03.2020** dismissing the suit were **upheld**.
- Counsel’s fee was assessed at **Rs. 2,500/-**.
- No interest directions were made.
- The court directed preparation of the decree sheet, return of the lower court record, and consignment of the appeal file to the Record Room.

## Core Issue

- Whether the appellant had proved, with reliable and consistent evidence, his entitlement to possession and injunctions over the disputed portion of property and the respondent’s alleged unauthorized possession.

## Key Facts

- The appellant, **Anubhav Walia**, sued claiming rights over **525 square yards** of land at Stadium Road, Patiala, said to form part of land originally linked to **Dera Garib Dass Ji**.
- He pleaded that the land had been leased in **1962** to **Narata Ram**, and that through subsequent inheritance and powers of attorney, he obtained rights to seek possession.
- He alleged that **Col. R.P.S. Brar** had forcibly कब्जा/occupied the suit land, constructed buildings, and let portions to other occupants.
- The respondents denied the claim, asserting that the appellant’s documents related to a different parcel and that the disputed property formed part of their own lawful leasehold chain.
- The defence also pleaded that the suit was barred by non-joinder, mis-joinder, res judicata, Order 2 Rule 2 CPC, limitation, lack of locus standi, and lack of cause of action.
- The trial court dismissed the suit on **04.03.2020**, leading to this appeal.
- During the appeal, respondent no. 2 remained ex parte.

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** The appellant failed to prove the exact identity, location, and boundaries of the suit property.
  **Evidence:** The court found that the plaint, GPA documents, lease documents, and site plan contained **different dimensions and directions**, and did not consistently identify the same property.
  **Impact:** Without precise identification, the claim for possession and injunction could not succeed.

- **Finding:** The appellant did not establish illegal possession by **Col. R.P.S. Brar**.
  **Evidence:** There was **no demarcation report** on record, and the oral evidence did not reliably prove when or how the respondent allegedly कब्जा/occupied the land.
  **Impact:** The alleged unauthorized possession remained unproved.

- **Finding:** The appellant’s own cross-examination weakened his case.
  **Evidence:** He admitted material gaps and inconsistencies regarding khasra/khewat/khatauni details and correction of boundaries.
  **Impact:** The court treated his evidence as insufficient to overcome the documentary contradictions.

- **Finding:** The draftsman’s evidence did not cure the defect in identification.
  **Evidence:** PW2 admitted he did not enter the property for measurement and had not shown key particulars properly in the site plan.
  **Impact:** The site plan was treated as incomplete and unreliable.

- **Finding:** The respondent’s evidence supported lawful possession and a separate title/lease chain.
  **Evidence:** DW3 and other defence witnesses produced lease/mutation material showing a different chain of possession and asserted continuous settled possession.
  **Impact:** This reinforced the court’s view that the appellant had not linked his documents to the respondent’s property.

- **Finding:** The trial court’s dismissal was not perverse or illegal.
  **Evidence:** The appellate court reviewed the oral and documentary evidence and found the trial court had properly appreciated it.
  **Impact:** No appellate interference was warranted.

## What This Case Means

- **For Property Litigants:** A claim for possession will fail if the plaintiff cannot prove the exact property with consistent title documents, boundaries, and measurement.
- **For Trial Lawyers:** If identity of land is disputed, a **demarcation report** can be critical; without it, oral assertions may carry little weight.
- **For Appellate Practitioners:** An appellate court will generally not interfere where the trial court’s findings are based on a rational appreciation of conflicting evidence.
