# Executive Brief

## Result

- The petitioners, led by **Manik Khurana**, lost: the High Court **dismissed CWP No. 39633 of 2025** and, by consequence, **CWP No. 39669 of 2025** and **CWP No. 39672 of 2025** were also dismissed.
- Relief sought against the Central Administrative Tribunal’s order dated **23.12.2025** was refused.
- No costs order is specified in the judgment.
- No interest directions are specified in the judgment.

## Core Issue

- Whether the UPSC could lawfully shortlist candidates for Assistant District Attorney interviews by raising the experience threshold above the advertised minimum when the application volume was very large.

## Key Facts

- The petitions arose from recruitment to **9 posts of Assistant District Attorney** in the Law and Prosecution Department, Chandigarh Administration.
- **Advertisement No. 08 of 2025 dated 28.06.2025** prescribed **2 years’ practice** as the minimum experience for applicants.
- The advertisement also stated that if applications were large, the Commission could adopt shortlisting criteria to restrict interview calls.
- **Manik Khurana** applied by **ORA dated 08.07.2025** and was within the field of applicants.
- The UPSC later issued a public notice dated **17.10.2025** increasing the experience benchmark for shortlisting to **6 years** for UR/OBC and **5 years** for EWS.
- The petitioner challenged that enhancement before the Central Administrative Tribunal in **O.A. No. 1157 of 2025**.
- The Tribunal rejected the interim stay request by order dated **23.12.2025**.
- The UPSC stated that it had received **1699 applications** for the **9 vacancies**, and only **138 applications** were taken into the consideration zone after shortlisting.

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** The shortlisting benchmark did not unlawfully alter the eligibility criteria.
  **Evidence:** The advertisement expressly allowed shortlisting where applications were large and specifically mentioned use of higher experience as a method.
  **Impact:** The Court treated the enhanced experience threshold as part of selection management, not an invalid change to eligibility.

- **Finding:** The UPSC’s action had a rational basis because the applicant pool was very large.
  **Evidence:** The Commission received **1699 applications** for only **9 posts**.
  **Impact:** The Court accepted that a screening benchmark was necessary to make interviews workable and effective.

- **Finding:** The Court found support in binding precedent permitting shortlisting by higher experience.
  **Evidence:** **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar, 1995 (1) SCT 50**; **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman, (1997) 4 SCC 664**; **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General Supreme Court of India, 2025 NCDHC 7483**.
  **Impact:** These authorities confirmed that a rational shortlisting benchmark is permissible if the rules or advertisement allow it.

- **Finding:** The petitioner failed to establish a prima facie case.
  **Evidence:** The Court noted no legal infirmity in the respondents’ shortlisting process and rejected the argument that the enhancement was inherently illegal.
  **Impact:** This defeated the request for interim or protective relief.

- **Finding:** The balance of convenience did not favour the petitioner.
  **Evidence:** The Court considered the recruitment process and shortlisting rationale, and saw no basis to interfere before final adjudication.
  **Impact:** The Tribunal’s refusal of interim relief was upheld and the writ petition was dismissed.

## Authorities Relied Upon

- **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar, 1995 (1) SCT 50**
  - Principle: Shortlisting by a higher experience threshold is permissible if it is rational and does not alter the basic eligibility prescribed by the rules or advertisement.
  - Application: Used to uphold the UPSC’s decision to call only more experienced candidates for interview.

- **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman, (1997) 4 SCC 664**
  - Principle: An employer may fix benchmarks at different stages of recruitment where the governing rules or advertisement permit it and the benchmark is not arbitrary.
  - Application: Cited to support the legality of raising the shortlisting standard here.

- **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General Supreme Court of India, 2025 NCDHC 7483**
  - Principle: Recruitment-stage benchmarks may be set if authorized and not arbitrary or illegal.
  - Application: Followed to reinforce the respondents’ discretion in shortlisting.

## What This Case Means

- **For Recruitment Authorities:** If the advertisement expressly reserves a right to shortlist, authorities may raise the interview-call benchmark to manage large applicant pools.
- **For Applicants:** Satisfying the minimum advertised qualification does not guarantee an interview when the advertisement allows rational shortlisting.
- **For Service-Law Practitioners:** Challenges to shortlist thresholds must show that the benchmark contradicts the rules/advertisement or is arbitrary, not merely that it is stricter than the minimum qualification.
- **For Employers:** A higher interview cutoff is defensible where it is used only as a screening device and not as a substitute for eligibility criteria.

## Operative Directions

- The petition is **dismissed**.
- **CWP No. 39669 of 2025** stands dismissed.
- **CWP No. 39672 of 2025** stands dismissed.
