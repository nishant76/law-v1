> **Winning Argument:** The respondent succeeded because the appellant failed to prove that the suit property described in his documents was the same property allegedly occupied by **Col.R.P.S. Brar**.

> **Key Takeaway:** A plaintiff seeking possession must prove his own title/entitlement and the precise identity of the property; inconsistencies in boundaries, khasra details, and site-plan evidence are fatal.

# Executive Brief

## Result

- The appeal by **Anubhav Walia** was **dismissed**.
- The judgment and decree dismissing the plaintiff’s suit were **upheld**.
- Reliefs for possession, mandatory injunction, permanent injunction, and use/occupation charges were not granted.
- Costs were awarded against the appellant.
- Counsel’s fee was assessed at **Rs.2,500/-**.
- No interest direction is specified in the judgment.

## Core Issue

- Whether the appellant proved that he was legally entitled to possession of the disputed land and that **respondent No.1** was in illegal possession of that specifically identified property.

## Key Facts

- The appellant claimed leasehold rights over land measuring **525 square yards**, allegedly derived from the leasehold rights of **Narata Ram** and his legal heirs.
- The appellant’s case was based on powers of attorney and lease documents allegedly executed through **Gurcharan Singh**.
- The appellant alleged that **Col.R.P.S. Brar** had unlawfully occupied the suit land, raised construction, and let out portions to other defendants.
- The respondent disputed the identity of the land and asserted that the appellant’s documents related to **property No.8135/5 / khasra No.429/2**, whereas the respondent’s property fell in **khasra No.429/1**.
- The respondent relied on registered lease deeds, mutations, municipal/house-tax records, and long possession to deny the appellant’s claim.
- The original suit had been dismissed by the trial court, leading to this first appeal.
- Respondent No.2 was proceeded ex parte in the appeal.
- The appellant had earlier deleted defendants No.2 to 4 from the suit after stating they were no longer in possession.

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** The appellant did not prove the correct identity, dimensions, or boundaries of the suit property.  
  **Evidence:** The court compared the plaint, **GPA Ex.P18**, **Mark-A**, and the appellant’s own admissions in cross-examination.  
  **Impact:** The appellant could not connect his documents with the property allegedly occupied by the respondent.

- **Finding:** The foundational documents relied upon by the appellant did not support his case.  
  **Evidence:** The court noted that **Mark-A** referred to a backside plot, contained different directions from **Ex.P18**, and neither document mentioned the relevant khasra/khewat/khatauni details.  
  **Impact:** The alleged authority of **Gurcharan Singh** to transfer or lease the disputed front portion was not established.

- **Finding:** Illegal possession by respondent No.1 was not proved.  
  **Evidence:** There was no demarcation report, and the appellant failed to prove when or how the respondent allegedly took possession.  
  **Impact:** The claim for possession and injunction failed.

- **Finding:** The site plan evidence was unreliable.  
  **Evidence:** **PW2 Indresh Khanna** admitted he had not entered the green-coloured area for measurement, had not shown title deeds, and had not properly calculated the uncovered area.  
  **Impact:** The site plan could not establish encroachment or the exact land in dispute.

- **Finding:** The appellant’s oral testimony contained material inconsistencies.  
  **Evidence:** In cross-examination, the appellant admitted absence of khasra/khewat/khatauni details in the power of attorney and no corrected document for changed boundaries.  
  **Impact:** His testimony was insufficient to discharge the plaintiff’s burden.

- **Finding:** The trial court had properly appreciated the evidence.  
  **Evidence:** The appellate court reviewed the oral and documentary record and found no illegality, ambiguity, or perversity.  
  **Impact:** No appellate interference was warranted.

## Authorities Relied Upon

- No reported case law was cited or applied in the judgment.
- The court applied the settled principle that a plaintiff must prove his own case by sufficient evidence and cannot succeed on weaknesses in the defendant’s case.
- The court applied that principle by requiring the appellant to prove legal entitlement, exact property identity, and illegal possession.

## What This Case Means

- **For Property Plaintiffs:** Possession suits based on leasehold rights require precise proof of property identity through consistent title documents, boundaries, and demarcation evidence.
- **For Property Defendants:** Long possession supported by revenue, lease, and municipal records can defeat a claim where the plaintiff cannot identify the suit property.
- **For Civil Litigators:** A site plan prepared without proper measurement or entry onto the disputed land may carry little evidentiary value.
