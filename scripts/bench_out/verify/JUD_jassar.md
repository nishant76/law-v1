> **Winning Argument:** The appellant failed to prove the suit property’s correct identification (dimensions/directions) and respondent’s alleged illegal possession because the relied GPAs/lease documents were internally inconsistent, lacked khasra/khatauni particulars, and no demarcation was proved.

> **Key Takeaway:** A plaintiff seeking possession/injunction based on lease/GPA rights must prove precise property identity (location/dimensions) and the defendant’s illegal possession through reliable documentary linkage and/or demarcation; oral assertions and an incomplete site plan will not suffice.

# Executive Brief

## Result
- **Appeal dismissed**; the **trial court judgment and decree dated 04.03.2020** dismissing the plaintiff’s suit were **upheld**.
- **Costs imposed** on the appellant.
- **Counsel’s fee assessed at Rs.2,500/-**.

## Core Issue
- Whether the appellant proved, on the basis of his asserted lease/GPA chain, a legally enforceable entitlement to **possession and injunctions** over the **specific land shown in green** in the site plan, including proof of its correct identity and the respondent’s illegal possession.

## Key Facts
- The appellant/plaintiff claimed leasehold rights traced to a **99-year lease (registered) dated 31.05.1962 (vasika no.344)** allegedly executed by Dera Garib Dass’s Mohatmim in favour of **Narata Ram** for land forming part of **khasra no.2281/429 (0-11)** (plaint narrative).
- After Narata Ram’s death, plaintiff relied on successive inheritance/mutations in favour of Narata Ram’s legal heirs (plaint narrative includes **mutation no.14851, 14852, 14854, 14855 dated 26.07.1988**).
- The appellant’s case: Narata Ram’s heirs allegedly executed **GPAs**; through attorneys, **525 sq. yds** was leased to **Surinder Krishan** (shown in red), and the “remaining” **525 sq. yds** (shown in green) was to be leased through **Gurcharan Singh**, who then executed a **GPA in favour of the appellant on 31.08.2005** and authorised him to take possession.
- Respondent No.1’s defence: the appellant’s documents relate to **khasra 429/2 (house no.8135/5)**, whereas respondent’s property falls in **khasra 429/1**; respondent claimed possession through lease transactions originating from **Hardial Singh’s sons** leasing out land on **04.02.1969** and subsequent leasing to respondent (including **lease deed dated 18.10.1977**), with supporting revenue/municipal tax documents (as pleaded and evidence referenced).
- Trial court framed issues including entitlement to possession, mandatory/permanent injunction, and use & occupation charges; after evidence, it **dismissed the suit on 04.03.2020**.
- The appellant filed the present first appeal on **08.07.2020**; **Respondent No.2 proceeded ex parte** in appeal.

## Court's Reasoning
- **Finding:** The appellant did not prove correct identity (dimensions/directions) of the suit property as pleaded.
  **Evidence:** The court compared **Ex.P18 (GPA dated 31.08.2005 executed by Gurcharan Singh in appellant’s favour)**, **Mark-A (POA relied upon; noted in judgment as executed by LRs of Narata Ram in favour of Gurcharan Singh)**, and the plaint; it found their **dimensions/directions do not tally**, and both documents lacked **khasra/khewat/khatauni particulars** though the plaint contained specific khasra details.
  **Impact:** Failure to establish the exact property defeated the claim for possession/injunction.

- **Finding:** Mark-A indicated Gurcharan Singh was given a **backside plot**, not the front portion abutting the main road.
  **Evidence:** The judgment notes Mark-A “clearly depict[s] the fact that plot of backside has been given to Gurcharan Singh”.
  **Impact:** Undermined appellant’s claim that Gurcharan Singh could confer rights over the disputed portion said to abut the main road / be in respondent’s possession.

- **Finding:** Absence of demarcation meant illegal possession could not be accepted on the appellant’s version.
  **Evidence:** The court records that **no demarcation was conducted before filing**, and though an **application for demarcation** was filed, it was **dismissed**; the appellant did **not appeal**, so the order became final.
  **Impact:** Without a demarcation report, the court declined to accept that respondent No.1 was in illegal possession of the specific disputed area.

- **Finding:** The appellant’s oral testimony contained material contradictions/admissions.
  **Evidence:** PW1 admitted (as recorded) that the POA relied upon contained **no khasra/khewat/khatauni**, that **corrections** were said to be made but **no separate correction document** was produced, and that no separate document was prepared correcting boundaries; he could not state whether revenue particulars were written in the relevant deed(s).
  **Impact:** Reduced reliability of appellant’s version on property identification and entitlement.

- **Finding:** The appellant’s site plan evidence was insufficient and incomplete.
  **Evidence:** PW2 (draftsman) admitted he had **not entered the suit property for measurement**, was not taken to the green area for measurement, did not show uncovered area calculation, and did not state that the red area was 525 sq. yds; he also stated he had not passed a draftsman diploma.
  **Impact:** The site plan **Ex.P16** did not prove exact dimensions/identity or illegal possession.

- **Finding:** The appellant withheld best available witnesses.
  **Evidence:** The court noted the appellant did not examine any of the **LRs of Narata Ram**, who were “best persons” to bring out the truth.
  **Impact:** Further weakened appellant’s case; supported affirmance of dismissal.

## What This Case Means
- **For property possession plaintiffs (lease/GPA-based claims):** If the foundational documents lack consistent boundaries/dimensions and cannot be reliably mapped to the pleaded khasra/property, the suit is likely to fail, especially without a demarcation report.
- **For defendants in boundary/title-link disputes:** Attacking the plaintiff’s property identification (contradictory dimensions, absence of khasra/khatauni particulars, lack of demarcation) can be dispositive.
