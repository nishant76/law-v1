# Executive Summary

## Purpose
- Provide a best-practices guide and checklist for planning and executing a **company-wide product launch**, covering **kickoff, development, roadmap management, early feedback/testing, cross-functional readiness (including legal), go-to-market/PR, onboarding/training, metrics, messaging, and launch execution**.

## Key Highlights
- Defines a product launch as a **planned and coordinated effort** to debut a new product and make it **generally available for purchase**, aiming to build anticipation, gather early feedback, and create momentum/industry recognition.
- Emphasizes “start with the end in mind”: establish a **high-level product vision** and **clear, measurable launch goals** with an expected time frame (the document lists example goals but does not prescribe specific timelines).
- Product launch kickoff:
  - Identify stakeholders early; schedule a **90-minute kickoff** and assign a note-taker.
  - Align on communication methods for a process that may run **months or years** before launch.
  - Focus on **value** (problems solved, alignment with company vision, target users) rather than early feature detail; acknowledge product limits/scope.
  - Conduct a **pre-mortem** to anticipate launch risks/missteps and prepare response plans.
- Product development: increase engineering engagement by sharing strategy/research, fostering ownership, and providing regular market/customer/competitor bulletins.
- Roadmap: treat as the **single source of truth**; avoid over-communicating dates early, but increase date precision as launch nears to avoid downstream timing failures across departments.
- Feedback/testing: encourage **employee usage during QA** (not only after acceptance/alpha) to uncover bugs, scalability issues, and improvement opportunities; stress-test all customer touchpoints (e.g., signup).
- Cross-departmental engagement:
  - Marketing: partner early to craft value proposition and go-to-market messaging; product management should review materials early for accuracy.
  - Sales: train on value proposition, ICP, realistic use cases; provide vetted collateral; enable sales-led demos; prepare FAQs/objection handling.
  - Support: train support to be product experts; establish an escalation and feedback process and documentation of user contact.
  - Legal: involve legal pre-launch to vet **customer contracts, terms & conditions, and legal/regulatory documentation**.
- Set the stage for success:
  - Build PR/go-to-market plan and be prepared to explain **pricing structure**.
  - Treat public beta as a “soft launch” to mine feedback; avoid delaying beta so long that feedback cannot be incorporated.
  - Implement scalable onboarding: defaults, training options, “train the trainers,” in-product prompts, accessible support documentation, and scalability strategy.
  - Post-launch nurturing: maintain momentum via assets and triggered communications (notifications/emails/prompts based on user behaviors).
- Metrics: align organization on success metrics tied to strategy (avoid vanity metrics); identify KPIs, automate reporting, watch red flags (e.g., churn/abandonment), and track on a regular schedule (weekly suggested).
- Messaging: develop and practice a **30-second and 2-minute elevator pitch**; stage announcements; choose channels based on buyer personas; pick an ideal launch date (potentially tied to events/holidays).

## Risks / Constraints
- Launch risk if stakeholders are not included early: misalignment and late-stage objections (“no one can claim they were unaware” if included from day one).
- Roadmap risk: if not kept current/accurate, launch activities can occur **too soon or too late**, causing lasting cross-department trust/coordination damage affecting future launches.
- Messaging risk: over-indexing on features rather than outcomes; attempting to say “everything at once” rather than staged messaging.
- Metrics risk: reliance on **vanity metrics** that do not align with organization KPIs.
- Legal/regulatory risk: involving legal only after launch can place the product “under legal pressure”; the guide stresses pre-launch vetting of contracts/T&Cs and regulatory documentation.
- Adoption risk: steep learning curve and poor onboarding/support documentation can cause users to churn before realizing value; inability to scale onboarding/training overwhelms product management.

## Action Items
- Define **high-level product vision** and **product launch goals** (clear, measurable, time-framed).
- Run a **product launch kickoff**:
  - Include/identify stakeholders; hold a **90-minute** meeting; assign a note-taker.
  - Set communications system for ongoing updates.
  - Align on product value, scope/limits, and themes; avoid feature-detail deep dives.
  - Conduct a **launch pre-mortem** and prepare contingency plans.
- During development:
  - Create a phased development approach; share strategy/research; empower engineering input.
  - Issue regular bulletins on customers, competitors, market dynamics, and potential deals.
- Maintain an accurate, regularly updated **roadmap** (potentially with different stakeholder versions).
- Accelerate feedback:
  - Have internal stakeholders (e.g., customer service) use the product during QA.
  - Stress-test customer/prospect touchpoints (e.g., signup, onboarding, trial flows).
- Cross-functional readiness:
  - Marketing: iterative value proposition and go-to-market review; early product accuracy review of collateral.
  - Sales: train on ICP/value proposition; provide vetted collateral; train sales demos; prepare FAQs/objection responses.
  - Support: train support; define escalation/feedback process; document user contacts.
  - Legal: pre-launch review of customer contracts, **terms & conditions**, and legal/regulatory materials.
- Set stage and execute:
  - PR and go-to-market campaign; pricing explanation plan.
  - Beta program targeted to likely buyers; incorporate feedback.
  - Onboarding/training content; “train the trainers”; in-product prompts; support documentation; scalability plan.
- Metrics and monitoring:
  - Agree success metrics tied to strategic goals; automate KPI tracking/reporting; monitor regularly; set schedule to reevaluate.
- Public messaging:
  - Practice **30-second** and **2-minute** pitch; stage announcements; select channels by persona; identify ideal launch date.
- Launch moment:
  - Execute release; celebrate and acknowledge contributors.
