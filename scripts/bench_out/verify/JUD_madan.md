> **Winning Argument:** The accused admitted his signature on the cheque and execution of an agreement acknowledging **₹18,00,000** liability, but failed to credibly prove alleged cash repayments, so the statutory presumptions of liability remained unrebutted.

> **Key Takeaway:** Once the drawer admits signature on the cheque, presumptions under **Sections 118(a) and 139 NI Act** operate and the accused must rebut them with a probable defence; unsupported claims of repayment (especially cash without proof) will not suffice.

# Executive Brief

## Result
- **Complainant won; accused convicted** for offence under **Section 138 NI Act**.
- The judgment records: **“Bonds u/s 437A CrPC furnished by the accused.”**
- **Sentence/compensation/costs/interest:** Not specified in the judgment.

## Core Issue
- Whether the accused rebutted the statutory presumptions by proving that his **actual outstanding liability was less than ₹18,00,000** (cheque amount) due to alleged repayments.

## Key Facts
- **Complainant:** **Madan Kumar**; **Accused:** **Rakesh Kumar Mishra**; parties were on “friendly terms” (as pleaded).
- Complainant alleged friendly loans: **₹4,50,000** (earlier) and **₹14,80,000** (around **November 2018**), totalling **₹19,30,000**.
- Complainant alleged accused agreed to pay an additional **₹1,20,000** towards expenses/interest/foreclosure penalty incurred due to complainant raising funds.
- Complainant alleged accused repaid **₹2,50,000**, leaving **₹18,00,000** due.
- Accused issued cheque **No. 046994** for **₹18,00,000** dated **01.05.2019**.
- Cheque dishonoured on **19.06.2019** with reason **“Funds insufficient”** (return memo).
- Legal demand notice dated **05.07.2019**; accused **admitted receipt** of the legal demand notice.
- Complaint instituted on **08.08.2019**.

## Court's Reasoning
- **Finding:** Admission of signature triggered presumptions under **Sections 118(a) and 139 NI Act** against the accused.  
  **Evidence:** Accused’s admission of signatures on **Ex. CW1/1** (at notice stage and in s.313) and reliance on **M/s Kalamani Tex v. P. Balasubramanian (2021) 5 SCC 283**.  
  **Impact:** Burden shifted to accused to rebut liability on a **preponderance of probabilities**.

- **Finding:** Defence that liability was less due to repayment (including **₹11,40,000** cash) was not credible/proved.  
  **Evidence:** Accused admitted **no receipt** for alleged **₹11,40,000** cash repayment, **no witness**, and (per court) beyond a mere suggestion, nothing was put to complainant regarding this payment.  
  **Impact:** Accused failed to create a probable defence; presumption remained.

- **Finding:** Accused’s execution of **agreement Ex. DW1/C1** expressly admitting liability of **₹18,00,000** contradicted his repayment story.  
  **Evidence:** Accused admitted executing **Ex. DW1/C1** and its contents acknowledging **₹18,00,000** liability.  
  **Impact:** Strengthened complainant’s case that **₹18,00,000** remained due; undermined defence.

- **Finding:** Claim that document **Ex. CW1/D1** was “forcibly executed” was not supported by any follow-up legal action.  
  **Evidence:** Accused admitted he filed **no civil suit** to stop operation of the document and made **no police complaint**.  
  **Impact:** Court treated the coercion claim as unsubstantiated, weakening defence credibility.

- **Finding:** Challenge to complainant’s financial capacity failed.  
  **Evidence:** Court notes complainant explained sources of funds in cross-examination and “nothing adverse could be further elicited.”  
  **Impact:** No dent to complainant’s version on ability to advance the loan amounts.

- **Finding:** Overall, accused failed to rebut the presumptions even on preponderance of probabilities.  
  **Evidence:** Court’s assessment of DW1 testimony and contradictions vis-à-vis documentary admission of liability.  
  **Impact:** Conviction under **Section 138 NI Act** followed.

## Authorities Relied Upon
- **Kusum Ingots & Alloys Ltd. v. K. Pennar Peterson Securities Ltd. (2000) 2 SCC 745**  
  Principle: Sets out the essential ingredients of offence under **Section 138 NI Act**.  
  Application: Used as the framework for evaluating whether the offence ingredients were met.

- **Rangappa v. Sri Mohan (2010) 11 SCC 441**  
  Principle: **Section 139** is a reverse-onus clause; accused must raise a probable defence on **preponderance of probabilities**.  
  Application: Guided the burden/standard for rebutting statutory presumptions after signature admission.

- **M/s Kalamani Tex v. P. Balasubramanian (2021) 5 SCC 283**  
  Principle: Once signature on cheque is established/admitted, reverse onus operates; accused must rebut presumption.  
  Application: Court applied it because accused admitted signatures on the cheque.

- **Kumar Exports v. Sharma Carpets (2009) 2 SCC 513**  
  Principle: Bare denial is insufficient; accused must bring on record probable circumstances showing non-existence of debt.  
  Application: Court used it to reject the accused’s unsupported repayment assertions.

## What This Case Means
- **For NI Act complainants:** Admission of signature plus a consistent dishonour/notice record can secure conviction unless the accused produces credible repayment/absence-of-liability proof.  
- **For NI Act accused/drawers:** Alleged cash repayments without receipts/witnesses, and inconsistent documentary admissions (like an agreement acknowledging liability) are unlikely to rebut presumptions.
