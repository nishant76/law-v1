> **Winning Argument:** The University terminated the petitioner’s employment strictly under the termination clause in his **03.08.2015** contractual appointment terms (carried into the **07.03.2019** ad hoc conversion), and the termination order **01.04.2025** was not punitive or FIR-based—so no prior notice/inquiry was required.

> **Key Takeaway:** A contractual employee’s services may be terminated in accordance with the contract terms, and writ interference is limited unless the action is shown to be mala fide, arbitrary, irrational, disproportionate, or unreasonable (Gridco).

# Executive Brief

## Result
- **Writ petition dismissed**: The High Court upheld the University’s termination order **dated 01.04.2025 (Annexure P-6)**.
- **No reinstatement / consequential benefits** were granted (the petitioner’s prayer to quash termination and continue in service was rejected).
- **Salary arrears**: Petitioner given **liberty to submit a representation** to the University for **arrears of salary**; the University must decide it within the timeframes set (see Deadlines).
- **Costs/interest**: Not specified in the judgment.

## Core Issue
- Whether a **contractual/ad hoc clerk** could be terminated **without notice or inquiry**, when the termination is **as per the contractual terms** and not by way of punishment.

## Key Facts
- The University advertised for **Clerk** posts in **2015**; petitioner applied and was selected by a Selection Committee.
- Petitioner appointed as Clerk on **contract** for **three months** via appointment letter **03.08.2015 (Annexure P-1)** on **consolidated salary**, with extensions from time to time.
- His appointment was converted to **ad hoc** by order **07.03.2019 (Annexure P-2)**, which stated that the **03.08.2015** appointment terms would be read as part of the ad hoc order.
- The University terminated his services on **03.02.2023 (Annexure P-3)** citing newspaper reports and **FIR No. 0032 dated 28.01.2023** under **Sections 370(5), 120-B, 417 IPC** and **Section 81, JJ Act, 2015** at **PS Sohana, SAS Nagar**.
- Petitioner challenged the **03.02.2023** termination in **CWP-13462-2024**; by order **16.09.2024 (Annexure P-4)**, the University treated that termination as withdrawn, with liberty to proceed afresh, and petitioner was to be reinstated within two weeks (subject to fresh proceedings).
- Petitioner was reinstated by order **22.10.2024 (Annexure P-5)**.
- The University again terminated his services w.e.f. **26.03.2025** via order **01.04.2025 (Annexure P-6)**, relying on **Condition No. 2** of the **03.08.2015** appointment letter.
- Present petition **CWP No. 13338 of 2025** challenged the **01.04.2025** termination primarily for want of **notice/inquiry**, and on the plea of **10 years’ service** and alleged retention of juniors.

## Court's Reasoning
- **Finding:** The petitioner remained governed by contractual terms allowing termination **without notice** even after ad hoc conversion.  
  **Evidence:** The **03.08.2015 (Annexure P-1)** terms (including termination without notice) were expressly incorporated into the **07.03.2019 (Annexure P-2)** ad hoc order.  
  **Impact:** The termination **01.04.2025** could be sustained if issued under those terms, without mandatory notice/inquiry.

- **Finding:** The **01.04.2025** termination was **not punitive** and was not founded on the FIR.  
  **Evidence:** The Court noted **Annexure P-6** did not refer to the FIR and was passed “in terms of condition mentioned in the appointment letter.”  
  **Impact:** Natural justice/inquiry requirements applicable to punitive action were not triggered on the Court’s reading.

- **Finding:** The earlier High Court order **16.09.2024 (Annexure P-4)** did not make an inquiry mandatory for the later termination order in the present form.  
  **Evidence:** **Annexure P-4** arose from the earlier FIR-based termination (**03.02.2023**), whereas **Annexure P-6** was contract-terms based and FIR-neutral.  
  **Impact:** Petitioner’s “mandatory inquiry” argument failed.

- **Finding:** The “last come, first go” principle and junior-retention argument did not assist a contractual employee.  
  **Evidence:** The Court held his employment was contractual and governed by contract terms.  
  **Impact:** Length of service (10 years) and alleged retention of juniors did not invalidate termination under contract.

- **Finding:** There was no merit in the argument that **Annexure P-3** and **Annexure P-6** were “virtually the same.”  
  **Evidence:** The Court distinguished **Annexure P-6** as “strictly in accordance with the terms and conditions” of the appointment letter.  
  **Impact:** The challenge to **Annexure P-6** on this ground was rejected.

- **Finding:** Termination of contractual employment as per contract terms is legally permissible; judicial review is limited.  
  **Evidence:** Reliance on **Gridco Limited v. Sri Sadananda Doloi, (2011) 15 SCC 16** (extract quoted).  
  **Impact:** The High Court found “no infirmity” in **01.04.2025** and dismissed the writ.

## Authorities Relied Upon
- **Gridco Limited and another v. Sri Sadananda Doloi and others, (2011) 15 SCC 16 (decided 16.12.2011)**  
  - Principle: Contractual termination as per contract terms is permissible; judicial review in contractual matters is limited to testing mala fides/arbitrariness/irrationality etc., and courts do not substitute their own view for the decision-maker.  
  - Application: Since **Annexure P-6** terminated employment under contractual terms (and was not punitive), the Court declined to interfere and upheld the termination.

## What This Case Means
- **For Universities/Public employers using contractual/ad hoc staff:** Termination aligned with a contractual termination clause is likely to be upheld in writ jurisdiction if it is not framed as punitive and the decision-making process is not shown to be arbitrary/mala fide.
- **For Contractual employees:** Long duration of service and junior-retention arguments may not help where employment is governed by a contract permitting termination without notice; arrears claims may need to be pursued by representation as directed.
