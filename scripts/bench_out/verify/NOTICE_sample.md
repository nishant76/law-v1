# Executive Summary

## Purpose
- Legal notice dated **31.01.2020** issued by **Prashant Sivarajan (Advocate, Supreme Court of India)** on behalf of **Mr. Kunal Kamra** to **InterGlobe Aviation Limited (IndiGo Airlines)** challenging IndiGo’s **6-month suspension/ban** on Mr. Kamra and demanding revocation, apology, compensation, and costs.

## Key Highlights
- The notice challenges IndiGo’s action as **illegal, arbitrary, and contrary** to the **Civil Aviation Requirements (CAR) Section 3 – Air Transport Series M Part VI Issue II dated 08.09.2017** and **principles of natural justice**.
- The notice asserts the ban was:
  - Publicised by IndiGo on Twitter on **28.01.2020 at 8:49 PM** (as per the notice), and
  - Communicated to Mr. Kamra by email on **29.01.2020 at 09:38 AM**.
- The notice alleges the CAR procedure was not followed, including:
  - No known **complaint from the Pilot-in-Command** (stated as required to trigger CAR process).
  - Matter allegedly **not yet referred to the Internal Committee** at the time of the notice.
  - Pending Internal Committee decision, any interim ban **cannot exceed 30 days** (citing CAR para 6.3), whereas IndiGo imposed **6 months**.
- The notice relies on a **DGCA tweet dated 29.01.2020 at 6:30 PM** stating (as quoted) that “**Now the matter is to be referred to the internal committee** … [and] the Internal committee is to give the final decision within **30 days** … binding on the airline concerned.”
- Reliefs demanded (para 24) include:
  - Immediate **revocation** of the 6-month suspension.
  - **Unconditional apology** in “all leading newspapers,” electronic media, and all IndiGo social media platforms.
  - **Compensation of Rs. 25,00,000/-** for alleged mental agony and losses from cancelled shows/programmes.
  - **Action against errant officials** responsible for imposing the ban, with reference to penalties under **Rule 133A** and **Aircraft Rules, 1937** (as described in the notice).
  - **Rs. 1,00,000/-** as cost of the legal notice.

## Risks / Constraints
- The notice alleges **procedural non-compliance** with the CAR (including Internal Committee process and interim ban limit), and **violation of natural justice** (no prior notice/opportunity to respond; no copy of complaint provided).
- The notice threatens “all possible legal recourse” including proceedings for alleged contravention of **Rule 133A of the Aircraft Rules, 1937** and references potential penalty (as described in the notice) of:
  - imprisonment up to **six months**, or
  - fine up to **two lakh rupees**, or
  - both.
- Factual assertions (e.g., absence of pilot complaint; absence of passenger complaints; acceptance of apology by crew/pilots) are stated from the client’s perspective in the notice; the document does not include IndiGo’s version or underlying complaints (if any).

## Action Items
- For IndiGo (as demanded in para 24):
  - Revoke the **6-month** suspension immediately.
  - Issue an unconditional apology (print/electronic/social media).
  - Pay **Rs. 25,00,000/-** compensation.
  - Initiate internal action against officials responsible (as demanded).
  - Pay **Rs. 1,00,000/-** notice costs.

## Key Takeaways
- The notice’s central position is that, under the cited **DGCA CAR (08.09.2017)**, (i) the matter should go to an **Internal Committee**, (ii) pending that decision any ban cannot exceed **30 days**, and (iii) the Internal Committee decides categorisation and duration of any ban—therefore IndiGo’s **6-month** ban is asserted to be ultra vires and void for **lack of due process**.
