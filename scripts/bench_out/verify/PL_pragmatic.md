# Executive Summary

## Purpose
- Provide a content-led framework (“blueprint”) for planning, executing, and measuring **B2B product launch marketing**, organized around the **buyer’s journey** rather than internal business priorities.

## Key Highlights
- The document states that product launch marketing often fails due to lack of a **coordinated launch marketing strategy** delivering a compelling product story “organized around the buyer, not the business.”
- It cites an **AcuPoll study**: “more than **95%** of new products launched by established companies each year fail.”
- Content is positioned as the “backbone” of launch success and should be used across the buying cycle and customer lifecycle; examples of content by stage include:
  - “Get the Buyer’s Interest”: blog posts, infographics, videos, presentations, live events
  - “Engage the Buyer”: emails/newsletters, whitepapers, online surveys, workbooks
  - “Align Product to the Buyer’s Needs”: webinars, live demos, product one-pagers, FAQs
  - “Secure the Buyer’s Confidence”: case studies, competitor differentiators, ROI calculators
- Planning guidance includes:
  - “Frame the problem” buyers are trying to solve (not just features).
  - Identify target audience and objectives using buyer personas (demographic + psychographic).
  - Example launch objectives listed: lead generation, revenue, adoption, awareness, beating a competitor (each with “how much/how many and by when” prompts).
- Provides a **6-step content framework** (Rebecca Kalogeris, Pragmatic Marketing):
  - Know the audience
  - Know how the audience makes a decision
  - Map available content and identify gaps
  - Know content preferences
  - Define content goals
  - Measure content effectiveness
- Recommends a “content pillar” model (three aligned assets across funnel stages):
  - “Appetizer” (top-of-funnel, engaging; drives to gated content)
  - “Entree” (main pillar asset like eBook/whitepaper/workbook; actionable, not product pitch; drives to product asset)
  - “Dessert” (product-centric asset like PDF/video/demo; supports sales enablement and deal acceleration)
- Channel distribution guidance includes a stage-by-stage grid (Awareness, Lead Generation, Nurture, Sales Enablement) with example content types and platforms.
- Encourages use of marketing technologies: email marketing tools, marketing automation, revenue tracking, CRM, content management platforms, Google Analytics/Adwords; includes a capability checklist (e.g., version control, multi-channel analytics, internal library).
- Execution focus: internal enablement (sales, customer success, marketing, PR) with specific asset lists; recommends an internal launch email with all go-to resources and a timeline of milestones.
- Post-launch: measure KPIs, gather internal and external feedback, and develop a **six-to-twelve-month** continued awareness plan; emphasizes early case studies/testimonials.

## Important Dates
- Not specified as calendar dates in the document.
- Time windows referenced:
  - Collect new-client feedback **“a few months after launch.”**
  - Build a continued awareness plan for **“six to twelve months immediately after launch.”**
  - Case study collection emphasis: **“the first six months to a year.”**

## Risks / Constraints
- High failure rate risk is emphasized (cited **>95%** failure statistic) where launches lack coordinated, buyer-led strategy.
- Operational risk: “dumping content” before buyers are ready can “waste time” and “may result in lost sales.”
- Execution risk: internal teams may be unprepared without enablement tools, centralized resources, and a milestone-based timeline (document frames internal enablement as “multi-step” requiring deadlines/milestones, but provides no fixed dates).

## Action Items
- Define buyer personas (demographic + psychographic) and list persona pain points; derive the launch messaging theme from those pain points.
- Set launch objectives (lead gen/revenue/adoption/awareness/competitive) and define metrics to track them.
- Create a content plan using:
  - The 6-step content framework (audience, decision process, content inventory/gaps, preferences, goals, measurement).
  - A content pillar (“Appetizer/Entree/Dessert”) and derivative assets to “feed every channel.”
- Build a launch calendar including **first draft due dates, final draft due dates, and final content launch dates** (the document instructs to include these items but does not provide specific deadlines).
- Prepare internal enablement assets by team (Sales, Customer Success, Marketing, PR) and distribute an internal launch email with links, talking points, templates, and resource storage location.
- On launch day, ensure public-facing communications are ready (blog post announcement, press release, social posts, promotional videos, email blasts).
- Post-launch:
  - Track KPIs (web traffic, MQLs/SALs, new revenue, etc.) via spreadsheet/dashboard and communicate insights internally.
  - Collect internal feedback on enablement/tools/outlets/timing; collect external feedback from new clients after a few months.
  - Implement a 6–12 month awareness plan and develop case studies/testimonials.

## Key Takeaways
- The blueprint is a **content-first, buyer-journey-aligned** approach: frame the buyer’s problem, build staged assets (Appetizer/Entree/Dessert), distribute across channels, enable internal teams, and then measure/iterate for months after launch.
