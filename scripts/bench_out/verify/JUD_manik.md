> **Winning Argument:** The UPSC’s advertisement expressly permitted shortlisting by adopting higher experience benchmarks where applications are large, and the enhanced experience thresholds (UR/OBC **6 years**, EWS **5 years**) were a rational shortlisting method rather than an unlawful change in eligibility.

> **Key Takeaway:** A recruiting authority may set higher experience benchmarks at the shortlisting stage (without changing baseline eligibility) if the rules/advertisement allow it and the benchmark is not arbitrary or illegal.

# Executive Brief

## Result
- **Manik Khurana (petitioner) lost**: **CWP No. 39633 of 2025** was **dismissed** on **29.12.2025**.
- The High Court upheld the **CAT Chandigarh** order dated **23.12.2025** dismissing the petitioner’s interim/stay application in **OA No. 1157 of 2025**.
- Consequence: no stay of the interview schedule; the petitioner was not granted provisional participation / keeping a post vacant (reliefs sought were not granted).
- **Connected writ petitions** **CWP-39669-2025** and **CWP-39672-2025** were also **dismissed**.
- **Costs/interest:** Not specified in the judgment.

## Core Issue
- Whether UPSC/selection authority can **enhance the experience criterion for shortlisting** candidates for interview beyond the minimum experience stated as an essential qualification in the advertisement.

## Key Facts
- UPSC issued **Advertisement No. 08 of 2025 dated 28.06.2025** for multiple posts including **9 posts of Assistant District Attorney (ADA), Chandigarh Administration** (reservation: **UR-07, EWS-01, OBC-01; PwBD-01** stated as reserved).
- Essential experience in the advertisement: qualified legal practitioner (Advocate) who has practiced for **2 years**.
- The advertisement included shortlisting provisions (Note-II) permitting shortlisting, including **“on the basis of higher experience… than the minimum prescribed”** if applications are large.
- Petitioner applied via ORA dated **08.07.2025**; his name appeared at **Sr. No. 578** among applicants.
- UPSC issued a **public notice dated 17.10.2025** adopting an enhanced-experience shortlisting criterion (petitioner’s case: UR experience raised to **6 years**), and called for documents from **27.10.2025**.
- Petitioner submitted a **representation dated 22.10.2025** objecting to enhancement of the experience criterion; no action was taken (as per pleading).
- Petitioner filed **OA No. 1157 of 2025** before CAT, Chandigarh, seeking stay/permission to participate; CAT rejected interim relief via **order dated 23.12.2025**.
- UPSC’s stand: **1699 applications** were received for **9 vacancies**; after adopting enhanced-experience shortlisting, **138 applications** were within the consideration zone.

## Court's Reasoning
- **Finding:** Shortlisting by adopting higher experience benchmarks is permissible in a large-applicant scenario.
  **Evidence:** The advertisement’s **Note-II** expressly allows shortlisting, including by **higher experience** than the minimum.
  **Impact:** The enhanced-experience criterion could not be attacked as per se beyond the advertisement.

- **Finding:** Enhancing experience for interview shortlisting does not amount to altering statutory/advertised eligibility when used only as a selection-stage filter.
  **Evidence:** Reliance on **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar, 1995(1) SCT 50** (SC) approving higher-experience shortlisting for interview where selection is interview-based and applications are large.
  **Impact:** Validated UPSC’s approach as part of the selection process, not an illegal substitution of eligibility.

- **Finding:** Employer may set benchmarks at different stages if permitted by rules/advertisement and not arbitrary/illegal.
  **Evidence:** **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman, (1997) 4 SCC 664**; followed in **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General Supreme Court of India, 2025 NCDHC 7483** (Delhi HC).
  **Impact:** Provided binding/supporting precedent that the shortlisting benchmark is legally sustainable if authorized and rational.

- **Finding:** On these facts, there was “nothing wrong” with the enhanced experience thresholds: **UR/OBC 6 years**, **EWS 5 years** (and PwBD reduced to 1 year, per UPSC’s written statement excerpt).
  **Evidence:** UPSC’s pleading of **1699** applications and adopted shortlisting formula; Court’s assessment that petitioner failed to show **prima facie case** or **balance of convenience**.
  **Impact:** No ground to interfere with CAT’s refusal of interim protection; writ petitions dismissed.

## Authorities Relied Upon
- **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar, 1995(1) SCT 50**
  - Principle: Shortlisting to restrict interview numbers can be done on a rational basis (including higher experience) without changing baseline eligibility.
  - Application: Supported UPSC’s enhanced-experience shortlisting for ADA interviews due to large applications.

- **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman, (1997) 4 SCC 664**
  - Principle: Benchmarks may be set at different recruitment stages if permitted and not arbitrary/illegal.
  - Application: Underpinned legality of stage-wise benchmarks in recruitment.

- **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General Supreme Court of India, 2025 NCDHC 7483**
  - Principle: Reiterates permissible stage-wise benchmarks where allowed by rules/advertisement and not arbitrary.
  - Application: Cited as consistent supporting authority for the same proposition.

## What This Case Means
- **For recruitment bodies/UPSC-type agencies:** If the advertisement/rules contain an enabling shortlisting clause, **higher experience thresholds** can be used to reduce interviewees where applications are excessive.
- **For candidates challenging shortlisting:** A challenge is unlikely to succeed unless the shortlisting benchmark is shown to be **unauthorized by the advertisement/rules** or **arbitrary/illegal** (mere increase over minimum eligibility is not enough).
