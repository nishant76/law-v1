> **Winning Argument:** PRTC was estopped from alleging rash/negligent driving in departmental proceedings after taking the stand before the MACT (and in its High Court appeal grounds) that **Harpal Singh** was **not negligent**, so the punishment order founded on that premise could not stand.

> **Key Takeaway:** An employer who has taken a specific position before a Tribunal that its driver was not negligent cannot later “change stand” to punish the same driver for negligence on the same accident; such disciplinary action is vulnerable as **illegal/null**.

## Core Issue
- Whether the disciplinary punishment for **rash/negligent driving** could stand when PRTC had taken the contrary position before the MACT that the driver was **not negligent**, and the punishment was based on the MACT award.

## Key Facts
- **Harpal Singh**, a **Driver (No. P-135)** with **PRTC, Patiala Depot**, was accused of causing an accident on **15.04.2014** by rash and negligent driving while on duty with **PRTC Bus No. 1356**.
- PRTC issued a charge sheet **Memo No. 4739 dated 22.09.2017** alleging rash/negligent driving.
- Departmental inquiry was conducted; show cause notice issued **18.04.2018**.
- Punishment order **No. 123 dated 18.05.2018** imposed: **stoppage of four annual increments with cumulative effect**.
- Departmental appeal was dismissed by order **No. 677 dated 29.03.2023**.
- Plaintiff filed civil suit seeking declaration that the charge sheet, punishment, and appellate order were illegal; suit dismissed by Civil Judge (Jr. Div.), Patiala on **13.12.2023**.
- Plaintiff appealed to District Judge, Patiala (**CIS No. CA/48/2024**); decision delivered on **27.02.2026**.

## Court's Reasoning
- **Finding:** PRTC had taken a clear stand before the **Motor Accident Claims Tribunal, Patiala** that the driver (**Harpal Singh**) was **not driving rashly and negligently**.
  **Evidence:** MACT pleadings exhibited (copy of written statement filed by PRTC marked **Ex.P2**).
  **Impact:** PRTC could not later proceed departmentally on the opposite allegation of negligence for the same accident; the disciplinary action became legally unsustainable.

- **Finding:** The case fell within the permissible scope of court interference in disciplinary matters where the order is vitiated on legal grounds (including “no evidence/legal evidence” principles as invoked).
  **Evidence:** Reliance on **Partap Singh v. State of Punjab and others, 2010(1) RSJ 503** (as cited in the judgment).
  **Impact:** The appellate court held it could examine validity/perversity and set aside the punishment.

- **Finding:** The punishment was effectively founded on the MACT award while PRTC itself disputed/contradicted negligence in MACT proceedings.
  **Evidence:** The judgment records that respondents “relied upon the award passed by the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal, Patiala,” while also having taken the non-negligence stand there.
  **Impact:** On the governing authorities, the punishment order was held **illegal, null and void**.

- **Finding:** Authorities on “cannot change stand” squarely applied.
  **Evidence:** High Court precedents discussed in the judgment (see Authorities section).
  **Impact:** Direct basis for allowing the appeal and granting declaratory relief and consequential benefits.

## Authorities Relied Upon
- **Partap Singh vs. State of Punjab and others, 2010(1) RSJ 503**
  - Principle: Courts do not sit in appeal over disciplinary evidence, but can interfere where it is a case of **no evidence/legal evidence** or where the inquiry/order is perverse/invalid.
  - Application: Enabled the court to scrutinize and invalidate the punishment in the present case.

- **Kulwant Singh Driver vs. Municipal Corporation, Amritsar and another, 2011(3) R.C.R. (Civil) 552**
  - Principle: Where the employer has taken a stand before MACT that the driver was not negligent, it cannot later impose recovery/punishment relying merely on MACT findings.
  - Application: Supported setting aside PRTC’s action premised on negligence after its contrary MACT stand.

- **Pepsu Road Transport Corporation vs. Harvinder Singh and others, RSA No. 2751 of 2013 (P&H), decided on 09.09.2013**
  - Principle: PRTC cannot be permitted to **change its stand** on driver negligence taken before the Tribunal.
  - Application: Treated as directly applicable; reinforced the conclusion that the punishment/charge sheet could not be sustained.

## What This Case Means
- **For Transport Corporations/Employers:** A litigation stance before MACT denying driver negligence can preclude later departmental action asserting negligence for the same accident; consistency of pleadings and disciplinary theory is critical.
- **For Employees facing disciplinary action:** If the employer has previously pleaded “no negligence” in connected tribunal proceedings, that can be a decisive ground to challenge subsequent negligence-based punishment.
