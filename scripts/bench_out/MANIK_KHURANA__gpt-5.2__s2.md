# Executive Brief

## Result

- **UPSC/Respondents won; petitioner lost.**
- **CWP No. 39633 of 2025** was **dismissed** on **29.12.2025**, and consequently **CWP-39669-2025** and **CWP-39672-2025** were also **dismissed**.
- The High Court upheld the **CAT order dated 23.12.2025** rejecting the petitioner’s stay application in **O.A. No. 1157 of 2025**.
- Costs/interest: **Not specified in the judgment.**

## Core Issue

- Whether UPSC could lawfully **enhance the experience benchmark for shortlisting** (from the advertised **2 years** practice) to restrict candidates called for interview.

## Key Facts

- UPSC issued **Advertisement No. 08 of 2025 dated 28.06.2025** for, inter alia, **9 posts of Assistant District Attorney (ADA), Chandigarh Administration**.
- Essential experience in the advertisement: **Advocate with 2 years’ practice**.
- The advertisement also contained a shortlisting clause (Note II) permitting shortlisting, including **“higher experience… than the minimum prescribed”** where applications are large.
- Petitioner **Manik Khurana** applied via ORA dated **08.07.2025**; his name appeared at **Sr. No. 578** in the applicants’ list.
- UPSC issued a **public notice dated 17.10.2025** raising the experience benchmark for interview shortlisting to **6 years** (UR/OBC) and required document submission from **27.10.2025**.
- Petitioner made a **representation dated 22.10.2025** challenging the enhanced experience criterion; no action followed.
- Petitioner filed **O.A. No. 1157 of 2025** before CAT and sought interim relief to stay interviews scheduled **29.12.2025 to 31.12.2025** (or permit him provisionally / keep one post vacant).
- CAT rejected the interim relief by **order dated 23.12.2025**, leading to the writ petition(s).

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** Shortlisting by raising the experience benchmark is permissible where the governing advertisement/rules allow it and the method is rational.
  **Evidence:** The court relied on the advertisement’s **Note II** expressly allowing shortlisting by **higher experience** when applications are large, and on Supreme Court precedents.
  **Impact:** This undercut the petitioner’s main contention that UPSC could not go beyond the advertised **2-year** experience for calling candidates to interview.

- **Finding:** The enhanced experience benchmark adopted here was not wrongful/illegal on the facts.
  **Evidence:** UPSC’s pleaded numbers: **1699 applications** for **9 vacancies**; shortlisting criteria applied leading to **138 applications** in the “consideration zone”; criteria included **UR/OBC: 6 years and above; EWS: 5 years and above; PwBD: reduced to 1 year**.
  **Impact:** The court treated the shortlisting as a legitimate selection-stage benchmark rather than an impermissible change in eligibility.

- **Finding:** Petitioner failed to satisfy interim-relief standards (no prima facie case / balance of convenience).
  **Evidence:** The court expressly recorded that counsel could not make out a **prima facie case** and that **balance of convenience** did not favour the petitioner.
  **Impact:** The CAT’s refusal to stay the interview process stood; the writ challenge failed.

- **Finding:** CAT’s order suffered from no “infirmity.”
  **Evidence:** The High Court’s application of the above legal position to the impugned **CAT order dated 23.12.2025**.
  **Impact:** Writ petitions were dismissed, leaving the recruitment process unaffected.

## Authorities Relied Upon

- **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar, 1995 (1) SCT 50**
  - Principle: In interview-only selections, a commission may shortlist on a rational basis (including higher experience) without altering minimum statutory eligibility.
  - Application: Supported treating UPSC’s raised experience as shortlisting, not a change to the **2-year** minimum eligibility.

- **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman, (1997) 4 SCC 664**
  - Principle: Employer can set benchmarks at different recruitment stages if permitted and not arbitrary/illegal.
  - Application: Reinforced validity of stage-wise enhanced benchmarks.

- **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General, Supreme Court of India, 2025 NCDHC 7483**
  - Principle: Following T. Sundararaman, benchmarks at different recruitment stages are permissible if rules/advertisement permit and the measure is non-arbitrary.
  - Application: Used to support the conclusion that UPSC’s shortlisting approach was within permitted parameters.

## What This Case Means

- **For recruitment/selection authorities:** If the advertisement/rules contain a shortlisting clause, you can apply **higher experience thresholds** to limit interview calls when applications are excessive, provided the measure is rational and non-arbitrary.
- **For candidates challenging shortlisting:** Courts are unlikely to interfere where the advertisement expressly authorizes shortlisting by higher experience and the authority shows an objective need (e.g., very high applicant-to-vacancy ratio).
