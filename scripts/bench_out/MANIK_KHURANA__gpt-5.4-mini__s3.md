> **Winning Argument:** The Court held that the UPSC’s decision to **shortlist by enhancing the experience benchmark** was a lawful part of the selection process, not an illegal change to the advertised eligibility conditions.
> **Key Takeaway:** Where the rules or advertisement permit it, a selecting agency may impose a rational shortlisting benchmark at the interview stage, including a higher experience threshold, without altering the underlying eligibility criteria.

# Executive Brief

## Result

- The petitioner, **Manik Khurana**, lost; the writ petition in **CWP No. 39633 of 2025** was **dismissed**.
- The Court upheld the Central Administrative Tribunal’s refusal to stay the interview process for recruitment to the post of **Assistant District Attorney**.
- No costs or interest directions are specified in the judgment.

## Core Issue

- Whether the **UPSC could lawfully enhance the experience requirement at the shortlisting stage** for interviews, even though the advertisement originally required only **2 years** of practice.

## Key Facts

- The petitioner and the connected petitioners were practicing advocates who applied for recruitment to **09 posts of Assistant District Attorney** in the Law and Prosecution Department, Chandigarh Administration.
- The UPSC issued **Advertisement No. 08 of 2025 dated 28.06.2025** setting the advertised experience requirement at **2 years** of practice as an advocate.
- The advertisement also stated that, if applications were large in number, the Commission could adopt shortlisting criteria to restrict the number called for interview.
- The petitioner submitted **Online Recruitment Application dated 08.07.2025** and was initially within the applicant pool.
- The UPSC later issued a public notice dated **17.10.2025** adopting a shortlisting criterion that raised the experience threshold to **6 years** for unreserved/OBC candidates and **5 years** for EWS candidates.
- The petitioner challenged this change before the Central Administrative Tribunal in **O.A. No. 1157 of 2025** and sought interim protection against the interview schedule.
- The Tribunal rejected the stay application by order dated **23.12.2025**.
- The writ petition challenged that order and sought to stop the interview process scheduled from **29.12.2025 to 31.12.2025**.

## Court's Reasoning

- **Finding:** The shortlisting exercise did not change the substantive eligibility criteria.
  **Evidence:** The Court relied on the advertisement’s note that the Commission could adopt shortlisting criteria where applications were large.
  **Impact:** This meant the UPSC’s later benchmark was treated as a permissible selection-stage filter, not an unlawful amendment to the advertisement.

- **Finding:** A recruiting body may set higher benchmarks at later stages if the rules or advertisement permit it and the exercise is not arbitrary.
  **Evidence:** The Court cited **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman (1997) 4 SCC 664** and **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General Supreme Court of India, 2025 NCDHC 7483**.
  **Impact:** This supplied the controlling principle that supported the UPSC’s shortlisting method.

- **Finding:** The Supreme Court’s decision in **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar, 1995 (1) SCT 50** directly supported shortlisting by enhanced experience.
  **Evidence:** That case approved calling only candidates with longer practice for interview when applications were excessive.
  **Impact:** The Court treated the present shortlisting as consistent with binding precedent.

- **Finding:** The petitioner did not establish a prima facie case or balance of convenience for interim relief.
  **Evidence:** The Court considered the Commission’s explanation that **1699 applications** were received against **9 vacancies** and that only **138 applications** remained in consideration after shortlisting.
  **Impact:** This defeated the request to stay the interview process or allow provisional participation.

- **Finding:** The impugned Tribunal order suffered from no infirmity.
  **Evidence:** The Court found the Commission’s action in raising the experience bar for shortlisting to be lawful and non-arbitrary.
  **Impact:** The writ petition was dismissed.

## Authorities Relied Upon

- **M.P. Public Service Commission v. Navnit Kumar Potdar, 1995 (1) SCT 50**
  - Principle: Shortlisting by a higher experience benchmark is permissible if it is rational and does not alter statutory eligibility.
  - Application: Used as the principal authority to uphold the UPSC’s filtering of candidates.

- **Union of India v. T. Sundararaman, (1997) 4 SCC 664**
  - Principle: An employer may set benchmarks at different recruitment stages when allowed by the rules or advertisement and not arbitrary.
  - Application: Cited to confirm that shortlisting criteria may differ from the minimum eligibility criteria.

- **Pramiti Basu v. Secretary General Supreme Court of India, 2025 NCDHC 7483**
  - Principle: The employer can impose stage-wise benchmarks if authorized and non-arbitrary.
  - Application: Followed to reinforce the permissibility of the Commission’s shortlisting method.

## What This Case Means

- **For recruitment authorities:** If the advertisement expressly allows shortlisting, a higher interview filter can be valid where candidate numbers are large.
- **For applicants:** Meeting the advertised minimum qualification does not guarantee an interview if a lawful shortlisting benchmark is later applied.
- **For service-law practitioners:** Challenges to shortlisting must show that the benchmark altered eligibility itself or was arbitrary, not merely that it was stricter.

## Operative Directions

- The petition is **dismissed**.
- **CWP No. 39669 of 2025** and **CWP No. 39672 of 2025** also stand **dismissed**.

## Immediate Next Steps

- If representing an applicant, review whether the advertisement expressly authorizes shortlisting by higher experience or other criteria.
- If challenging a shortlisting decision, focus on demonstrating that the authority changed eligibility rather than merely filtered candidates.
- If advising a recruiting body, ensure the advertisement contains clear shortlisting language and the benchmark has a rational nexus to selection.
- Confirm whether the applicant pool size and selection mechanism were recorded contemporaneously to justify the benchmark.
- Check whether any connected writ petitions or tribunal proceedings require separate follow-up, although the judgment states they are dismissed.
